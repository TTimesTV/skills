# User-approved chapter density and wording

## Editorial intent

The user explicitly approved the list below after rejecting an overly dense 13-chapter list and a too-sparse 7-chapter revision for a 34:37 interview. Their guidance: fewer than 10 body chapters for a video of this length, without repeatedly splitting it into 1–2 minute sections.

Select substantial topic transitions, then write concrete titles that explain what the viewer can learn there. The approved result has nine chapters, mostly around three to five minutes apart. These are calibration observations, not mandatory intervals. Short ad/highlight segments do not count toward body density.

## What improved

- `시리·제미나이·챗GPT·클로드, AI 관문은?` became `시리·제미나이 vs 챗GPT·클로드, 누가 AI의 관문이 될까?`. The user explicitly said this passage should naturally have been framed as `vs`. It compares device/OS-side assistants against established AI services for the user's primary AI interface. Preserve those two sides and the stake instead of listing four brands as peers. Apply this principle when the source actually contains a competition or comparison; do not force every title into a rivalry.
- `기업용 AI의 관문과 데이터 보안` became `회사의 모든 정보를 AI에 맡겨도 될까?`, with the boundary moved to the actual security discussion at 08:37.
- `자체 GPU 시대, 엔비디아의 AI 모델 전략` became `엔비디아가 직접 AI 모델을 만드는 이유`.
- `한국 AI 기업의 사업 모델 변화` became `GPU를 매출로 바꾸는 국내 AI 기업들`.
- `프라이빗 AI가 바꿀 빅테크 판도` became `엔비디아·MS·아마존의 판도는 어떻게 바뀔까?`.
- The 25:00 chapter moved to the substantive corporate GPU transition at 26:55, removing the short 22:50–25:00 interval. This decision followed the transcript, not an arbitrary spacing rule.

Concrete wording matters as much as chapter count. Avoid mechanically alternating questions, padding a list to nine, or merging distinct topics solely to reduce the total. Keep each title faithful to the actual passage and retain exact CC-derived boundaries.
